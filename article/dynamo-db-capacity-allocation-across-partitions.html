DynamoDB Capacity Allocation Across Partitions

Amazon DynamoDB manages read and write throughput by distributing capacity across underlying physical storage partitions. While provisioned capacity is fundamentally split evenly among all partitions, DynamoDB utilizes intelligent mechanisms—namely burst capacity and adaptive capacity—to dynamically redistribute unused throughput to disproportionately active partitions. This ensures that uneven workloads do not cause unexpected throttling as long as the total table-level limits and single-partition physical limits are not exceeded.

Partition Fundamentals in DynamoDB

DynamoDB tables are divided into physical storage units called partitions. Partitions are managed entirely by AWS and are provisioned automatically based on two criteria:

  • Data Size: Each partition can hold a maximum of 10 GB of data.
  • Throughput Limits: A single partition can support a maximum of 1,000 Write Capacity Units (WCUs) or 3,000 Read Capacity Units (RCUs).

When a table exceeds either the 10 GB storage threshold or the throughput threshold of an individual partition, DynamoDB automatically allocates additional partitions to distribute the load.

Initial Capacity Distribution

When throughput is provisioned for a table, DynamoDB calculates the initial allocation on a per-partition basis using a simple formula:

\[\text{Partition Capacity} = \frac{\text{Total Provisioned Capacity}}{\text{Number of Partitions}}\]

For example, if a table has 10 partitions and is provisioned with 1,000 WCUs and 3,000 RCUs, each partition is nominally allocated 100 WCUs and 300 RCUs.

If capacity allocation remained strictly static, any partition receiving traffic higher than its equal share would experience throttling, even if the total table consumption remained well below the provisioned threshold. DynamoDB implements two features to prevent this issue: burst capacity and adaptive capacity.

Burst Capacity

DynamoDB reserves a portion of unused capacity to absorb brief spikes in traffic. If an application does not consume all of its provisioned throughput, DynamoDB retains up to 300 seconds (5 minutes) of unused read and write capacity in a burst buffer.

Partitions that experience sudden, short-lived surges in traffic can tap into this burst pool to fulfill requests without throwing a ProvisionedThroughputExceededException. However, burst capacity is temporary and will deplete quickly if sustained traffic exceeds the partition's baseline allocation.

Adaptive Capacity

To handle sustained uneven access patterns (often referred to as "hot partitions"), DynamoDB employs Adaptive Capacity. Adaptive capacity works automatically in the background for all tables at no additional cost.

Dynamic Throughput Balancing

Adaptive capacity monitors the real-time consumption of individual partitions. If a specific partition experiences sustained traffic exceeding its nominal share, DynamoDB automatically reallocates unused throughput from quiet ("cold") partitions to the active ("hot") partition.

As long as the total consumption across all partitions does not exceed the overall provisioned capacity of the table, the hot partition can consume significantly more than its standard slice of throughput.

Partition Splitting for Hot Keys

If a single partition experiences intense access targeting a specific partition key, adaptive capacity can isolate that key. DynamoDB will split the partition, moving the heavily accessed key to its own dedicated partition. This prevents a single high-traffic item from starving other items of capacity and enables the hot key to utilize up to the physical maximum of a single partition (1,000 WCUs or 3,000 RCUs).

Hard Limits of Single Partitions

While DynamoDB manages capacity dynamically, it cannot violate physical hardware boundaries. Regardless of overall table capacity or adaptive redistribution, a single partition key can never exceed:

  • 1,000 Write Capacity Units (WCUs) per second
  • 3,000 Read Capacity Units (RCUs) per second (or 6,000 eventually consistent reads)

If the access pattern on a single partition key surpasses these physical boundaries, requests will be throttled regardless of available capacity elsewhere in the table.

Capacity Management in On-Demand Mode

In On-Demand capacity mode, DynamoDB does not require provisioned throughput settings. Instead, it instantly accommodates workloads up to previously reached traffic peaks.

DynamoDB manages partition allocation in On-Demand mode by automatically increasing partition counts as overall volume grows. If current traffic exceeds twice the previous peak, DynamoDB automatically creates new partitions to scale up capacity while continuing to use adaptive capacity to route throughput dynamically to where it is needed most.
