DynamoDB Auto Scaling CloudWatch Metrics

Monitoring Amazon DynamoDB helps maintain application performance and optimize provisioned throughput costs. This article explains which Amazon CloudWatch metrics reveal whether your DynamoDB auto scaling policies require adjustment. By analyzing metrics like throttling events and capacity utilization, you can identify scale-out delays, inappropriate capacity limits, and overly aggressive scaling parameters to ensure your database handles workload changes smoothly.

The Key Metric: ThrottledRequests

The most critical CloudWatch metric for determining if your auto scaling policy needs adjustment is ThrottledRequests (along with its operation-specific counterparts, ReadThrottleEvents and WriteThrottleEvents).

When a DynamoDB table receives more read or write requests than its current provisioned capacity can support, it throttles the excess requests. DynamoDB auto scaling uses target tracking, which takes several minutes to detect elevated traffic, trigger a CloudWatch alarm, and scale up provisioned capacity. If you observe recurring spikes in ThrottledRequests during sudden traffic surges, your auto scaling policy is not responding aggressively enough to workload changes.

Capacity Metrics: Consumed vs. Provisioned Throughput

To understand the context of throttle events, compare ConsumedReadCapacityUnits (or ConsumedWriteCapacityUnits) against ProvisionedReadCapacityUnits (or ProvisionedWriteCapacityUnits).

DynamoDB calculates utilization as:

\[\text{Utilization} = \frac{\text{Consumed Capacity Units}}{\text{Provisioned Capacity Units}} \times 100\]

Reviewing consumed versus provisioned units helps you spot two primary policy issues:

  • Hitting Maximum Limits: If consumed capacity reaches the Provisioned limit and remains flat while throttles increase, your auto scaling policy has likely hit its defined upper limit (Maximum Provisioned Capacity).
  • Target Mismatches: If consumed capacity regularly nears 100% of provisioned capacity before auto scaling triggers, your target utilization percentage is configured too high to absorb rapid traffic changes.

How to Adjust Your Auto Scaling Policies

Once you analyze these metrics, apply targeted adjustments to your auto scaling settings:

  • Lower the Target Utilization: The default target utilization is 70%. If sudden traffic surges cause ReadThrottleEvents or WriteThrottleEvents, reduce the target utilization to 50% or 60%. This provides a larger capacity buffer while auto scaling reacts.
  • Raise Maximum Capacity Limits: If provisioned capacity flats out at your configured ceiling during peak hours, increase the maximum capacity units in the scaling policy.
  • Adjust Cooldown Periods: Scale-out cooldowns determine how quickly consecutive scale-up activities can occur. Reducing the scale-out cooldown allows DynamoDB to add capacity more quickly during sustained traffic growth. Conversely, increasing the scale-in cooldown prevents capacity from dropping too rapidly during momentary lulls in bursty workloads.
