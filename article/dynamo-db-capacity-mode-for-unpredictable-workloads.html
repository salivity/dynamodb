DynamoDB Capacity Mode for Unpredictable Workloads

Choosing the right Amazon DynamoDB capacity mode is critical for controlling cloud costs, especially when application traffic fluctuates wildly. For unpredictable or sporadic workloads, DynamoDB On-Demand capacity mode is the most cost-effective solution. This article explains how On-Demand mode compares to Provisioned mode, why it optimizes spending for irregular traffic patterns, and how to determine if it fits your specific use case.

Understanding DynamoDB Capacity Modes

Amazon DynamoDB offers two pricing models for processing reads and writes:

  • Provisioned Capacity Mode: You specify the exact number of Read Capacity Units (RCUs) and Write Capacity Units (WCUs) your application requires per second. You pay for these provisioned units on an hourly basis, regardless of whether your application consumes them.
  • On-Demand Capacity Mode: You do not pre-allocate throughput. Instead, DynamoDB automatically scales up or down to accommodate incoming request volumes. You pay strictly per request (per million read and write request units).

Why On-Demand Is Best for Sporadic Workloads

For workloads with irregular spikes, sudden quiet periods, or completely unknown usage patterns, On-Demand capacity mode offers distinct financial and operational advantages.

1. Zero Cost for Idle Time

Sporadic applications often sit idle for hours or days before experiencing sudden bursts of activity (e.g., batch processing, event-driven webhooks, or internal tools). In Provisioned mode, you continue paying for allocated RCUs and WCUs even when zero traffic hits the database. On-Demand mode charges purely per request, meaning idle hours cost zero dollars.

2. Elimination of Over-Provisioning

With unpredictable traffic, Provisioned mode forces developers to size the database for peak demand to avoid ProvisionedThroughputExceededException errors. Sizing for the peak results in massive waste during normal or low-traffic periods. On-Demand mode eliminates this trade-off by scaling dynamically to match any load instantly.

3. Limitations of Auto-Scaling in Provisioned Mode

While Provisioned mode supports Application Auto Scaling, it reacts too slowly for sharp, unpredictable spikes. Auto-scaling relies on Amazon CloudWatch alarms that can take several minutes to evaluate metrics and trigger capacity increases. If an unpredictable surge occurs in seconds, auto-scaling will result in throttled requests unless significant buffer capacity is maintained—which increases costs. On-Demand mode adapts instantly to traffic changes without throttling, provided the surge does not exceed double the previous peak within a short window.

Cost Comparison: On-Demand vs. Provisioned

  • Predictable, Steady Traffic: Provisioned mode (especially paired with DynamoDB Reserved Capacity) is typically cheaper if your application runs at a steady, predictable baseline with high utilization rates (consistently above 60–70%).
  • Spiky, Sporadic Traffic: On-Demand mode is significantly cheaper because the unit cost per request, while higher on paper than an ideally saturated provisioned unit, avoids the heavy penalty of paying for unutilized capacity over time.

Key Takeaway

If your application experiences fluctuating, bursty, or unpredictable request volumes, switch to DynamoDB On-Demand capacity mode. It eliminates capacity planning, prevents request throttling during unexpected surges, and ensures you only pay for the exact resources your database consumes.
