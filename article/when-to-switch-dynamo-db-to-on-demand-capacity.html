When to Switch DynamoDB to On-Demand Capacity

Choosing the right billing mode for Amazon DynamoDB directly impacts application performance and cloud infrastructure costs. This article outlines the specific scenarios and workload characteristics that indicate it is time to switch a table from provisioned capacity to on-demand mode, including unpredictable traffic spikes, low-utilization patterns, and developer overhead reduction.

Unpredictable or Spiky Traffic Patterns

The primary indicator for switching to on-demand mode is traffic that fluctuates rapidly without notice. DynamoDB's provisioned auto-scaling requires several minutes to respond to sudden traffic spikes, often resulting in ProvisionedThroughputExceededException errors during the lag time. If your application frequently experiences immediate, sharp surges in reads or writes that cause request throttling, on-demand capacity instantly accommodates these bursts without manual intervention or pre-warming.

New Applications with Unknown Usage Profiles

When launching a new application, microservice, or feature, estimating read capacity units (RCUs) and write capacity units (WCUs) is challenging. Starting with or switching to on-demand capacity allows you to establish a baseline of real-world traffic patterns without risking under-provisioning outages or over-provisioning expenses. Once historical CloudWatch metrics demonstrate consistent, predictable trends, you can re-evaluate whether returning to provisioned mode makes financial sense.

Low, Intermittent, or Idle Workloads

Provisioned mode charges for capacity allocated per hour, regardless of whether that capacity is consumed. If your workload involves periodic batch jobs, dev/test environments, or applications that remain mostly idle outside of business hours, provisioned capacity incurs wasted spend. On-demand mode charges strictly per request, reducing baseline costs to zero for periods with no database activity.

High Operational and Management Overhead

Managing provisioned capacity requires configuring target utilization percentages, scaling thresholds, upper and lower bounds, and monitoring alarms across multiple tables and global secondary indexes (GSIs). If your engineering team spends significant time tuning auto-scaling parameters to prevent throttling, switching to on-demand eliminates capacity planning and maintenance overhead, freeing resources to focus on core product development.

Utilization Consistently Below 60–70%

From a pure cost perspective, on-demand requests cost more per individual read and write than heavily utilized provisioned capacity. However, if your provisioned table maintains an average utilization rate below 50% to 60%—often done deliberately to provide a safety buffer against spikes—on-demand is typically the more cost-effective choice. Review your CloudWatch ConsumedReadCapacityUnits and ConsumedWriteCapacityUnits against your provisioned limits; if the gap between consumed and provisioned is consistently wide, on-demand will likely lower your monthly bill.
