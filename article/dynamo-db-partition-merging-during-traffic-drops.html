DynamoDB Partition Merging During Traffic Drops

When application traffic to Amazon DynamoDB drops significantly, the database adjusts its internal resource distribution to maintain performance and avoid throttled requests. While DynamoDB historically retained its partition count indefinitely once split, modern DynamoDB architecture employs automatic partition merging alongside adaptive capacity to consolidate underutilized partitions. This ensures that provisioned and consumed throughput adapts dynamically without stranding capacity across fragmented partitions.

Partition Allocation Fundamentals

DynamoDB distributes table data and throughput across multiple physical partitions. Each individual partition can support a maximum of:

  • 10 GB of data storage
  • 1,000 Write Capacity Units (WCU)
  • 3,000 Read Capacity Units (RCU)

When a table exceeds any of these thresholds, DynamoDB splits the partition into two or more new partitions, dividing both the data keyspace and the provisioned throughput evenly among them.

Historical Behavior: The One-Way Partition Split

Historically, partition splitting in DynamoDB was a strictly one-way operation. If an application experienced a high-traffic event (such as a flash sale) that caused DynamoDB to scale up to 100 partitions, the table remained split across 100 partitions even after traffic dropped back to baseline levels.

Under this legacy behavior:

  • Total provisioned throughput was divided equally across all active partitions (for example, 1,000 WCU across 100 partitions yielded only 10 WCU per partition).
  • A subsequent uneven access pattern could cause individual partitions to throttle, even if the total table-level throughput was far below the allocated limit.

Modern Behavior: Automatic Partition Merging

To eliminate the inefficiencies of permanent partition splitting, AWS introduced automatic partition merging. When table storage decreases (e.g., via bulk deletes or Time to Live) or when read/write traffic decreases significantly over an extended period, DynamoDB merges adjacent partitions.

During a traffic drop, the partition reallocation process works through the following stages:

  1. Usage Monitoring: DynamoDB’s control plane continuously tracks partition-level metrics, identifying partitions whose combined storage and sustained throughput fall well below the limits of a single partition.
  2. Background Consolidation: DynamoDB initiates a non-disruptive, asynchronous background process to merge adjacent key ranges.
  3. Throughput Reassignment: As partitions merge, their assigned throughput limits combine. This prevents provisioned capacity from being diluted across an unnecessarily large number of partitions.
  4. Zero-Downtime Cutover: Routing routers update their partition key maps atomically, ensuring ongoing reads and writes experience no downtime or elevated error rates during the transition.

The Role of Adaptive Capacity

Partition merging takes time to evaluate and execute because it is designed to optimize long-term resource efficiency rather than react to temporary dips. To handle short-term drops and localized spikes, DynamoDB relies on Adaptive Capacity.

If traffic drops overall but remains concentrated on a small subset of partitions, Adaptive Capacity automatically shifts unused throughput from cold partitions to warm partitions. This ensures that even before partition merging completes, uneven traffic distributions do not trigger false throttling errors.

Impact Across Capacity Modes

The handling of partition resources when traffic drops varies slightly depending on the table's billing mode:

  • On-Demand Mode: DynamoDB automatically tracks peak traffic and accommodates drop-offs without manual tuning. If traffic drops, DynamoDB does not throttle; it simply charges for the exact requests made while gradually consolidating partitions in the background if consumption remains low.
  • Provisioned Mode: If traffic drops but you keep your provisioned throughput high, the capacity remains assigned across the existing partition topology. If you lower your provisioned capacity settings (manually or via Application Auto Scaling), DynamoDB downscales total allocated capacity and triggers partition consolidation if the lower throughput no longer justifies the current partition count.
