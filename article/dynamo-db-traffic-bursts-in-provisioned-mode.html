DynamoDB Traffic Bursts in Provisioned Mode

Amazon DynamoDB utilizes built-in mechanisms like burst capacity and adaptive capacity to handle sudden spikes in read and write requests when operating in provisioned mode. This article explains how DynamoDB dynamically redistributes throughput, banks unused capacity, and coordinates with Application Auto Scaling to absorb brief traffic spikes without immediate throttling.

Burst Capacity

DynamoDB reserves a portion of your unused provisioned throughput to accommodate sudden bursts of read or write activity. When your workload operates below its allocated Read Capacity Units (RCUs) or Write Capacity Units (WCUs), DynamoDB banks the unused capacity for up to 300 seconds (5 minutes).

If a sudden spike in traffic exceeds your baseline provisioned limit, DynamoDB automatically consumes this banked burst capacity. This process is seamless and temporary; once the 300-second pool of accrued capacity is depleted, requests will face throttling if the elevated traffic rate persists.

Adaptive Capacity

In provisioned mode, a table’s throughput is divided across underlying physical partitions. Without adaptive capacity, an uneven distribution of requests (a "hot partition") could exceed an individual partition’s allocated slice and trigger throttling, even if the overall table has unused capacity.

DynamoDB solves this through adaptive capacity, which works in two main ways:

  • Throughput Redistribution: DynamoDB automatically shifts unallocated or unused throughput from quiet partitions to hot partitions. This allows a single partition to consume more than its default share of capacity as long as the aggregate table limit is not breached.
  • Hot Partition Isolation: If a specific item or partition experiences sustained, disproportionate traffic, adaptive capacity can automatically isolate that partition onto dedicated hardware to protect neighbor partitions and maintain consistent overall throughput.

Coordination with Auto Scaling

While burst capacity covers spikes lasting seconds to minutes, Application Auto Scaling handles sustained traffic growth. When configured, auto scaling monitors Amazon CloudWatch utilization metrics and dynamically raises the table's provisioned RCUs and WCUs when consumption crosses a predefined target utilization percentage.

During the delay between a sudden burst and the point at which auto scaling can provision new capacity (typically a few minutes), burst capacity acts as the buffer, shielding the application from ProvisionedThroughputExceededException errors.

Throttling Boundaries

Burst handling in provisioned mode is designed for short-lived, transient traffic fluctuations. If the burst lasts longer than 300 seconds, or if the spike exceeds the physical throughput limits of a single partition (1,000 WCUs or 3,000 RCUs) without sufficient adaptive rebalancing, DynamoDB will reject excess requests. Applications should still implement exponential backoff and jitter in their client configurations to handle any requests that breach these boundaries.
